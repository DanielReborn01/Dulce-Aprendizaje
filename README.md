### 1. Introduction

This report details the development of the practical workshop focused on kinematic modeling, open-loop control, and simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM) algorithms, all using the Dingo-O mobile robot. The project is structured in the following two complementary phases:

* **Implementation of Proprietary Kinematic Control:** Development of speed change and inverse kinematics functions responsible for calculating the required angular velocities in each of the four mecanum wheels in sel_controller.py.
* **SLAM Mapping and Navigation:** Robot teleoperation using the integrated Clearpath controller (cmd_vel), integrating a LiDAR sender, slam_toolbox and Nav2 in a simulated environment in Gazebo, with the aim of generating the map.

### 2. Development of the Technical Questionnaire

#### 2.1 Differences between kinematic models: Differential and Omnidirectional

The fundamental difference between the two schemes lies in the geometric constraints and the degree of holonomy that restricts the state space in the **SE(2)** plane:

* **Degrees of Freedom and Holonomy:** The differential robot is non-holonomic, meaning it has a non-integrable kinematic constraint. To change its lateral trajectory, it must pivot. In contrast, the omnidirectional robot, thanks to its mecanum wheels, is holonomic, allowing it to decouple its angular orientation from its translation in the plane.
* **Mapping and Inverse Kinematics:** In the differential scheme, two actuators are controlled based on the linear forward speed and the rotational speed. The Dingo-O robot uses four mecanum wheels with **45°** rollers, linked through the inverse kinematics matrix:

**ω_fl** = (1 / r) · [ v_x − v_y − (l_x + l_y) · ω ]  
**ω_fr** = (1 / r) · [ v_x + v_y + (l_x + l_y) · ω ]  
**ω_rl** = (1 / r) · [ v_x + v_y − (l_x + l_y) · ω ]  
**ω_rr** = (1 / r) · [ v_x − v_y + (l_x + l_y) · ω ]  

* **Geometric parameters of the Dingo-O:** r = 0.05 m (radius), l_x = 0.285 m, l_y = 0.1854 m.
* **Contact and Traction Mechanics:** The differential model transmits direct, parallel forces to the wheel via longitudinal static friction. The mecanum wheels transmit forces at a **45°** angle through multiple passive rollers, making the chassis movement the result of a vector sum of the force vectors generated by the four wheels.

#### 2.2 Comparison and Advantages between Kinematic Models

| Criterion / Characteristic | Differential Model | Omnidirectional Model (Mecanum) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Maneuverability in Tight Spaces** | Limited; requires reversing maneuvers and wide radius turns in narrow aisles. | Superior; instant lateral translation capability (crabbing) and diagonal displacement without rotating the chassis. |
| **Mechanical Complexity and Maintenance** | Low; fewer components (2 main motors), solid wheels and easy assembly. | Larger; requires 4 independent motors with encoders and wheels with bearings and rollers at **45°** subject to wear. |
| **Tolerance for Irregular Terrain** | High; excellent traction on ramps, uneven surfaces and rough or outdoor terrain. | Low; very sensitive to bumps and uneven surfaces; irregularities cause loss of traction on individual rollers. |
| **Open Loop Odometer Accuracy** | Moderate to High; lateral slippage is minimal under moderate accelerations. | Low; continuous slippage inherent to the **45°** rollers, generating rapid cumulative drift without external correction. |
| **Energy Efficiency** | High; the torque of the actuators translates directly into forward thrust without opposing vector components. | Moderate; some of the energy is dissipated in opposing vector components needed to cancel unwanted forces. |

#### 2.3 Proposal for the Application of the Omnidirectional Model

* **Proposed Application:** Autonomous Mobile Robot (AMR) for Intralogistics and Picking in High Density Warehouses (Pharmaceutical and Hospital Sector).
* **Technical and Operational Justification:** In pharmaceutical distribution centers, the aisles between vertical storage racks are very narrow (between **1.0 m** and **1.3 m** wide) to optimize square footage. Robots with differential drive face significant challenges when docking perpendicularly to the supply trays, requiring time-consuming Y-turns that increase the risk of collision. The Dingo-O omnidirectional robot can move along the aisle and dock directly sideways (pure **90°** crabbing) in front of the loading station without needing to adjust the pitch angle or rotate the chassis, drastically reducing cycle time per pick.

#### 2.4 Implementation of the Proposal in a Local Environment (Ecuador)

To deploy this solution in a viable and sustainable way in the Ecuadorian industrial context, three strategic pillars must be considered:

1. **Industrial Surface Conditioning:** Since Mecanum wheels are severely affected by irregularities or dust, installation should focus on warehouses with level floors and self-leveling epoxy coating (for example, in industrial parks in Guayaquil, Durán, or the Metropolitan District of Quito). This mitigates abrasive wear on the **45°** rollers and prevents loss of traction.
2. **Robust Sensory Fusion Strategy (Low Cost):** The accumulation of odimetric drift in mecanum wheels requires the robot to be independent of encoders. Locally, a ROS 2 Jazzy-based architecture is proposed, featuring a slam_toolbox assisted by accessible 2D LiDAR scanners and an array of AprilTags / QR codes installed on ceilings or shelves for millimeter-precise absolute pose correction.
3. **Local Component Manufacturing and Replacement:** To avoid Clearpath's high tariffs and import times for spare parts, the structural chassis can be manufactured locally using CNC machining and laser cutting of aluminum sheets in domestic metalworking shops. The mecanum wheel rollers can be manufactured using injection molding or 3D printing in engineering materials (Nylon PA12 or high-density TPU).

#### 2.5 Technical and Operational Recommendations

* **Acceleration and Jitter Control:** Configure trapezoidal and smooth acceleration profiles in the cmd_vel speed publisher. Abrupt speed changes induce inertial slippage in the mecanum rollers, generating severe discrepancies between the actual and estimated trajectory.
* **Preventive Maintenance Protocol:** Establish weekly cleaning inspections of the bearings of each of the **45°** rollers. The accumulation of lint, dust, or metal shavings blocks the free rotation of the passive rollers, degrading the omnidirectional kinematics to erratic behavior.
* **ROS 2 use_sim_time configuration:** In simulations with Gazebo Harmonic and ROS 2 Jazzy, rigorously verify that all active nodes (self_controller, slam_toolbox, rviz2) have the parameter use_sim_time:=true. Otherwise, desynchronizations occur in the tf buffer, causing localization drops.
* **Closed-Loop Mapping Strategy:** During the mapping phase with Nav2/slam_toolbox, remotely control the robot at low speed (v ≤ 0.2 m/s) and ensure closed circular paths (loop closures). This allows the graph optimizer to correct odimetric drift in a timely manner before persisting the .yaml and .pgm maps.
